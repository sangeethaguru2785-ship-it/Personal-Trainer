Nutrition

Nutrition Guide

Understand what your body needs to perform at its best.

Protein-Rich Foods

Chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, and legumes. Aim for 1.6–2.2g per kg of bodyweight for muscle growth.

Healthy Carbohydrates

Oats, brown rice, sweet potatoes, quinoa, and whole-grain bread. Carbs fuel your workouts and recovery.

Healthy Fats

Avocados, nuts, olive oil, salmon, and chia seeds. Essential for hormones, brain function, and absorption of vitamins.

Vitamins & Minerals

Eat the rainbow — colorful fruits and vegetables provide micronutrients that support immunity and recovery.

Fiber-Rich Foods

Vegetables, fruits, beans, and whole grains. Fiber supports digestion, keeps you full, and stabilizes blood sugar.

Calorie Balance

Understand your TDEE. Eat in a slight surplus to build muscle, or a moderate deficit to lose fat — consistency is key.

Maintenance Eat equal to what you burn to stay steady

Try This: Barbell Deadlift

Benefits

  • Builds full-body strength — back, glutes, hamstrings, core
  • Improves posture and spinal stability
  • Boosts testosterone and growth hormone naturally
  • Enhances grip strength and athletic performance

Instructions

  1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, barbell over mid-foot

Myth vs Fact

Separating Truth from Fiction

? Myth

Sweating more burns more fat.

? Fact

Sweating is mainly your body's cooling mechanism, not a direct measure of fat loss. You can burn fat without breaking a sweat.

? Myth

Skipping meals helps you lose weight faster.

? Fact

Skippping meals often leads to overeating later. Regular, balanced meals keep your metabolism stable and hunger in check.

? Myth

Cardio is the only way to burn fat.

? Fact

Strength training builds muscle, which raises your resting metabolic rate — burning more calories 24/7 than cardio alone.

? Myth

You need to train every day to see results.

? Fact

Rest days are when your muscles repair and grow. Overtraining leads to burnout and injury. 3-5 focused sessions beat 7 mediocre ones.
